Notes on Differential Privacy

2024-02-15 Research

These are some notes I've compiled while studying differential privacy and its applications in machine learning. Differential privacy is a formal framework for quantifying privacy guarantees in statistical databases and machine learning.

What is Differential Privacy?

Differential privacy provides a mathematical definition of privacy that can be used to analyze and verify privacy guarantees in algorithms that operate on sensitive data.

Formal Definition

A randomized mechanism M satisfies ε-differential privacy if for all adjacent datasets D and D' (differing in exactly one element) and for all subsets S of the output space:

Pr[M(D) ∈ S] ≤ e^ε × Pr[M(D') ∈ S]

where ε (epsilon) is the privacy budget - a smaller ε means stronger privacy.

Key Mechanisms

Laplace Mechanism

For numerical queries, we can add Laplace noise calibrated to the sensitivity of the query:

noise ~ Lap(0, Δf/ε)

where Δf is the sensitivity (maximum change in output between adjacent datasets).

Exponential Mechanism

For selecting the best output from a discrete set:

Pr[output = x] ∝ exp(ε × q(D, x) / 2)

where q is a quality score function.

Applications in Machine Learning

Differential privacy has important applications in machine learning:

  • Private Training Data - Training deep learning models while protecting training data privacy
  • Federated Learning - Combining differential privacy with federated learning frameworks
  • Private Inference - Ensuring predictions don't leak information about training data

Trade-offs

There's typically a trade-off between privacy and utility:

  • Lower ε → Stronger privacy → More noise → Lower utility
  • Larger datasets can achieve better privacy-utility trade-offs

"Privacy is not an option, it's an approach." — This is especially true in the age of AI.
